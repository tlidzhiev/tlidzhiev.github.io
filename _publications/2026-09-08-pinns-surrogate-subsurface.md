Inverse problems and uncertainty quantification in subsurface flow modeling typically require repeated high-fidelity hydrodynamic simulations for different values of uncertain model parameters. In realistic reservoir modeling settings, such simulations are computationally expensive, making extensive exploration of the parameter space impractical. Surrogate models are therefore commonly employed; however, their construction still relies on sufficiently rich training data, and adequate parameter-space coverage remains challenging. As a result, learning from a limited number of parameter samples is a realistic and practically relevant scenario. In this work, we investigate Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) as surrogate models in the low-data regime. Unlike conventional neural networks, PINNs incorporate the governing partial differential equations, together with initial and boundary conditions.
